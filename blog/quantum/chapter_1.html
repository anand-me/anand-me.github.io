What Is a Computer?
From Human to Machine

A long time ago, a "computer" was actually a person! These people did math calculations by hand. Later, machines took over the job. Now, we have tiny computers in our pockets—smartphones—that are more powerful than the computers that helped land astronauts on the moon! 🚀

Understanding Bits
Coins and Probability

Imagine flipping a coin. It can land on Heads (H) or Tails (T) - that's 2 possible outcomes.

  • 2 coins: 2 × 2 = 4 outcomes (HH, HT, TH, TT)
  • 3 coins: 2³ = 8 outcomes
  • n coins: 2ⁿ outcomes
Dice and Information

A regular die has 6 sides (1 to 6).

  • 2 dice: 6 × 6 = 36 outcomes
  • 3 dice: 6³ = 216 outcomes
  • n dice: 6ⁿ outcomes

💡 Key Insight

Anything with 2 states (like a coin) carries the smallest amount of information. That's what a bit is!

Physical Bits

Bits are everywhere in the physical world:

  • 💡 Light switch: Off (0) or On (1)
  • 💿 DVD: Pit (0) or Land (1)
  • ⚡ Circuit: 0 volts or 5 volts
Binary Numbers

Binary uses only 0s and 1s. Each position represents a power of 2:

Binary:   1   0   1   1
Powers:   8   4   2   1
Sum: 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11

Binary to Decimal Method

Each binary digit represents a power of 2:

Example: 1011₂
1 × 2³ = 1 × 8 = 8
0 × 2² = 0 × 4 = 0
1 × 2¹ = 1 × 2 = 2
1 × 2⁰ = 1 × 1 = 1
Total: 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11₁₀
Decimal to Binary Method

Find the largest power of 2 that fits, then subtract and repeat:

Example: 13₁₀
13 ≥ 8 (2³) → Yes, use 1 → 13 - 8 = 5
5 ≥ 4 (2²) → Yes, use 1 → 5 - 4 = 1
1 ≥ 2 (2¹) → No, use 0
1 ≥ 1 (2⁰) → Yes, use 1 → 1 - 1 = 0
Result: 1101₂
