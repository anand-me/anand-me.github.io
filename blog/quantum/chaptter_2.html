Qubit Touchdown: A Quantum Board Game

Imagine a football game where the ball moves in strange ways—not just forward or backward, but to weird places like "plus," "minus i," or "square root of X." That's what the game Qubit Touchdown is like! 🏈

Think of the football as a qubit. The way it moves on the board is like how a qubit changes when we apply different quantum gates.

🌟 What Makes Qubits Special?

Unlike classical bits that are either 0 or 1, qubits can be BOTH at the same time! This magical property is called superposition.

Classical vs Quantum Bits

Classical Bit: Either 0 or 1 (like a light switch - on or off)

Quantum Bit (Qubit): Can be |0⟩, |1⟩, or BOTH simultaneously!

  • |0⟩ (read as "ket zero")
  • |1⟩ (read as "ket one")
  • |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 (superposition state)
  • |−⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2 (another superposition)

🌍 The Bloch Sphere

We visualize qubits on a sphere where:

• North Pole = |0⟩ • South Pole = |1⟩ • Equator = Superposition states

Superposition: The Quantum Magic
The Spinning Coin Analogy

Superposition is like spinning a coin—it's not just heads or tails, it's both until you look! 🪙

When you measure a qubit in superposition, it "collapses" to either |0⟩ or |1⟩ with certain probabilities.

🌀 Common Superposition States

|+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 → Equal chance of 0 or 1

|−⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2 → Also equal chance, but different phase

|i⟩ = (|0⟩ + i|1⟩)/√2 → Using imaginary numbers!

Complex Numbers in Quantum

Quantum mechanics uses complex numbers with real and imaginary parts:

Example: 1 + i√3
Real part: 1
Imaginary part: √3

Two forms:
• Cartesian: x + iy
• Polar: r·e^(iθ)

Quantum Measurement
Measurement in the Z-Basis

When you measure a qubit, it stops being in superposition and becomes either |0⟩ or |1⟩. The probability depends on the amplitudes (the numbers in front of |0⟩ and |1⟩).

Example: If a qubit is in state 0.6|0⟩ + 0.8|1⟩:

  • Probability of measuring |0⟩ = |0.6|² = 0.36 = 36%
  • Probability of measuring |1⟩ = |0.8|² = 0.64 = 64%

⚖️ Normalization Rule

The total probability must always add up to 1 (100%). Sometimes we need to adjust the numbers to make sure this happens!

|α|² + |β|² = 1

Measurement in Other Bases

You can measure qubits in different directions:

  • Z-basis: |0⟩ and |1⟩ (standard measurement)
  • X-basis: |+⟩ and |−⟩ (diagonal measurement)
  • Y-basis: |i⟩ and |−i⟩ (circular measurement)

Each gives different results depending on the qubit's state!

Consecutive Measurements

🔍 First measurement: Collapses the qubit to |0⟩ or |1⟩

🔍 Second measurement (same basis): Always gives the same result

🔍 Measurement in different basis: Can give a different result!

The Bloch Sphere

🌍 Visualizing Qubits in 3D

The Bloch sphere helps us visualize any qubit state in 3D space!

Spherical Coordinates

We can describe a qubit's position using two angles:

  • θ (theta): How far down from the north pole (0 to π)
  • φ (phi): How far around the equator (0 to 2π)
|ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^(iφ)sin(θ/2)|1⟩
Cartesian Coordinates

You can also use x, y, z coordinates on the sphere:

  • x = sin(θ)cos(φ)
  • y = sin(θ)sin(φ)
  • z = cos(θ)

🎯 Key Bloch Sphere Points

North Pole (0,0,1): |0⟩ state

South Pole (0,0,-1): |1⟩ state

+X axis (1,0,0): |+⟩ state

-X axis (-1,0,0): |−⟩ state

+Y axis (0,1,0): |i⟩ state

-Y axis (0,-1,0): |−i⟩ state

Global vs Relative Phase

Global Phase: Changes that don't affect measurements (invisible)

Relative Phase: Changes that DO affect measurements (important!)

Only relative phase matters for quantum computing! 🎭

Quantum Gates

🧰 What Are Quantum Gates?

Quantum gates are operations that rotate and transform qubits on the Bloch sphere. They're like magical tools that change the qubit's state!

X

Pauli-X Gate

The Bit Flip: Flips |0⟩ ↔ |1⟩

Like a classical NOT gate but for qubits!

Y

Pauli-Y Gate

The Twist: Flips and adds phase

Rotates around Y-axis on Bloch sphere

Z

Pauli-Z Gate

The Phase Flip: Changes sign of |1⟩

|0⟩ → |0⟩, |1⟩ → -|1⟩

H

Hadamard Gate

The Superposition Maker: Creates |+⟩ from |0⟩

The most important gate in quantum computing!

S

S Gate (Phase)

The Quarter Turn: 90° rotation

Adds i phase to |1⟩ component

T

T Gate

The Eighth Turn: 45° rotation

Fine-tuned phase adjustment

Reversibility

All quantum gates are reversible! This means:

  • You can always undo any quantum operation
  • Information is never lost in quantum computing
  • Every gate has an inverse gate
